Scope

Focused by design.

A fast fit check before you book time. F‑Link earns its place where global air APIs hand off, and as the air layer for TMCs already on G‑Link.

F‑Link is built for
  • TMCs with Asia or China-route exposure: domestic, outbound, or APAC with regional carriers.
  • TMCs already on G‑Link who want air through the same vendor, contract, and mid-office.
  • Teams building AI booking agents that need Travelsky-routed air via the MCP API surface.
  • TMCs where Travelsky access is the gap their current global API can't close.
F‑Link is not built for
  • Global all-route coverage as the headline. If China and APAC aren't in your route mix, F‑Link won't be the biggest lever you can pull today.
  • Leisure-first OTAs. F‑Link routes corporate-travel-grade air. Wrong shape of customer.
  • US-domestic-only TMCs with no Asia exposure. Your existing global air API will outperform us on those lanes.
  • "Replace everything" projects. F‑Link augments. Most TMCs multiplex air APIs, that's how we expect to be deployed.

Treat it as a focused extension of a mature platform, not a standalone air startup. The scope is intentionally clear.

Air bookings flow into the same mid-office that runs G‑Link hotel events: cancellations, reschedules, refunds, and traveler emergencies. One operational stack, one escalation path.

SLA terms are scoped per deployment. In the demo, G‑Link's production numbers set the reference floor.

Skills is the fastest path: paste a command into your AI tool, describe the air flow, and generate against the F‑Link MCP surface. Working prototype in hours, not weeks.

Traditional API or SDK cutover is typically 2–6 weeks, depending on the booking pipeline. TMCs already on G‑Link can cut that roughly in half with shared auth, login, and docs.

Pricing uses a setup fee plus a monthly platform subscription, tiered by product line. The G‑Link bundle is the most efficient air entry for TMCs already integrated.

Commercial terms are scoped against projected volume. The value is China-route access and bundled operations, not price-per-ticket.
